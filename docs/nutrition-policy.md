# Nutrition target policy

Version `daily-2026-09-v1` uses a confirmed body weight of 30–300 kg. It is a planning heuristic for this single-user fencing app, not an individualized dietetic prescription. Missing weight or an unrecognized goal blocks personalized targets. Historical dates use wearable observations through the prior day; forecasts use observations through yesterday in the athlete's configured timezone.

Daily protein is 1.8 g/kg for performance, maintain, and lean bulk, and 2.0 g/kg for cutting and recomposition. Carbohydrate is 3, 4, 5, 6, or 6 g/kg for rest, gym, fencing, double, or competition days respectively. Fat starts at 0.8 g/kg. Garmin's preceding 14 days of valid calorie readings are averaged if at least five days are present; otherwise the existing 38 kcal/kg local estimate is used. The latter is a provisional app assumption, not a measured expenditure. The selected goal requests 100% of baseline for performance, maintain, or recomp, 95% for cutting, and 105% for lean bulk. Remaining energy is allocated first to fat up to 1.5 g/kg, then to carbohydrate up to 1 g/kg above the day base. Protein and training carbohydrate have priority when that cannot meet the energy request. The displayed kcal is always `4 × protein grams + 4 × carbohydrate grams + 9 × fat grams`, rounded to a whole kcal; macro grams are rounded to 0.1 g. The API reports any unresolved energy conflict.

Version `competition-2026-09-v1` previews seven days before the selected event, every inclusive event day, and the first day after. Days 7–3 before the start retain ordinary targets. For moderate or high expected activity demand, days 2 and 1 before use a preparation carbohydrate floor of 3.5 and 4.0 g/kg (moderate), or 4.0 and 4.5 g/kg (high). A rest day remains a rest training day. Event days use one event carbohydrate target of 5, 6, or 7 g/kg for low, moderate, or high expected active demand; no second competition multiplier is stacked. The first day after uses at least 4 g/kg only after high-demand events. Protein and fat follow the daily policy, and kcal is recalculated from macros. These are daily planning targets, not claims that all venue hours are active exercise. An unknown start time is labelled as an assumption; expected active demand must be entered. Overlapping event contexts require explicit selection. No automatic endurance carbohydrate-loading course is prescribed.

Worked example: a 75 kg athlete on an ordinary rest day starts at 225 g carbohydrate. The day before a high-demand event has at least 337.5 g while training remains rest. A high-demand event day has 525 g carbohydrate. Protein at 1.8 g/kg is 135 g; with 0.8 g/kg fat, the event-day macro energy floor is `135×4 + 525×4 + 60×9 = 3180 kcal`. The displayed target may rise above the requested energy when the event carbohydrate floor takes priority.

The ranges and demand-based approach are informed by the [ACSM/Academy/DC joint position statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26891166/), [AIS training and competition nutrition](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/education-modules/modules/training-and-competition-nutrition), [AIS sports drinks guidance](https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_a/sports-foods2/sports-drink/how-and-when-do-i-use-it), [Sports Dietitians Australia pre-exercise guidance](https://www.sportsdietitians.com.au/factsheets/pre-exercise-fuelling/), and [USOPC athlete plates](https://www.usopc.org/nutrition). AIS describes 30–60 g/hour during 1–2.5 hours of endurance or stop-and-start exercise, and USOPC distinguishes easy, moderate, and hard days. These sources do not validate a fencing-specific daily macro algorithm, a 38 kcal/kg expenditure formula, or the app's exact event-day increments; those remain disclosed local policy choices. Event-day during-exercise intake and hydration require individual practice and context.
